eBay condition description examples (and how to describe flaws)

The short answer: a good eBay condition description gives the grade, then lists every flaw with four things: what it is, where it is, how big it is, and which photo shows it. For example: "Good pre-owned condition. Light bobbling under both arms. Pale mark on the front below the left pocket, about 5 mm, see photo 6. No holes or stains that we can see." Facts, not adjectives. Copy the examples below and swap in your own details.

Why condition wording matters

Buyers mind surprises far more than flaws. A clear condition description sets the right expectation and shows you disclosed the flaw if a question comes up. "Sold as seen" does not rescue a wrong description: even private sellers must describe items accurately, and business sellers have extra duties under UK consumer law (general information, not legal advice).

The four-part flaw formula

For every flaw, write one line with:

  1. What: mark, pull, bobbling, fading, chip, scratch, dent, stain, fault.
  2. Where: front, back, left cuff (say "as worn"), rim, top casing.
  3. How big: in mm or cm. Put a coin or ruler in the photo for scale.
  4. Which photo: "see photo 5".

Then add what you checked and found fine: "No holes, all buttons present, zip works."

Weak versus strong wording

"Barely noticeable" is your opinion. A size lets the buyer decide.

Electronics condition examples

Never write "fully working" unless you tested every function. Say what you tested and what you did not.

Used, tested and working: powers on, reads discs, connects to Wi-Fi, controller pairs and charges. Two light scratches on the top surface (photos 4 and 5). Factory reset and signed out of all accounts. Not tested: Bluetooth headset.

GUC meaning on eBay (and other abbreviations)

You will see these a lot on eBay and Vinted:

AbbreviationMeaning
BNWTBrand new with tags
BNWOTBrand new without tags
NWTNew with tags
EUCExcellent used condition
VGC or VGUCVery good condition, or very good used condition
GUCGood used condition

GUC is fine as shorthand, but it is still an opinion. Spell it out and follow it with the flaw list: "GUC" alone tells a buyer nothing about the mark on the cuff.

Match the condition field too

eBay's condition dropdown varies by category. Read eBay's definitions for yours when you choose, and check the current options in eBay's help pages. Two rules help:

  • If you are between two grades, pick the lower one. A buyer who gets better than expected is happy. One who gets worse opens a case.
  • The grade never replaces the flaw list. The dropdown and the description should agree.

FAQ

How do you describe flaws on eBay without putting buyers off? Plainly and specifically. A flaw with a size and a photo reads as honest. Price to match the condition and let the buyer decide.

Should I photograph every flaw? Yes. Every flaw in the text should be in a photo, and the other way round. Use a coin or ruler for scale.

Is "used" enough for a condition description? No. "Used" covers worn once to falling apart. Give the grade, then the flaws.
