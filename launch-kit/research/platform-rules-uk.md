# Platform rules and UK law: fact sheet for Well Listed writers

Last checked: 30 September 2026 (fact-check session; first research session 29 September 2026). Items confirmed on 30 September are marked "VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026)".

This sheet is what product and marketing writers may rely on when they mention a platform rule, a limit, a fee or a legal point. Anything not on this sheet, or marked "not verified", must not be stated as fact. Every platform fact must go out with "check the current rule in the platform's help pages" (WRITING-RULES.md rule 9).

## How to read the confidence labels

- **VERIFIED (official):** confirmed from the platform's or regulator's own page (help centre, seller centre, newsroom, gov.uk). In this session the official pages could not be opened directly (the network blocks direct page fetches), so these were confirmed from the official page's own text as returned in search results. The official URL is given so it can be opened and rechecked.
- **SECONDARY:** reported consistently by two or more independent third-party sources (seller tools, trade press such as ChannelX, Value Added Resource, Retail Dive) but not seen on an official page this session. Safe for internal planning. In customer-facing copy, soften ("at the time of writing, eBay allows up to...") and add the "check the current rule" line.
- **NOT VERIFIED:** could not be confirmed this session. The figure given is background knowledge for orientation only. Do not publish it until someone checks the linked official page.

## Headline warnings for the product team (read first)

1. **Etsy does not allow AI prompt bundles to be sold.** Etsy's Seller Handbook article "What is Etsy's stance on AI creations?" says lists of prompts for generative AI may not be sold on Etsy. The Well Listed Kit is largely AI prompts, so it should not be listed on Etsy as a prompt pack. SECONDARY leaning official: the rule appears in text quoted from Etsy's own handbook page (https://www.etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004) and in several independent 2026 guides (e.g. https://www.promptlesspress.com/blog-etsy-ai-policy-2026-digital-products). Recheck the handbook page before any Etsy decision.
2. **TikTok Shop UK treats digital downloads as unsupported.** Digital media and digital downloads are reported as unsupported products on TikTok Shop UK (a US invite-only virtual goods programme exists; the UK does not appear to have one). SECONDARY: https://tslagency.co.uk/can-you-sell-digital-products-on-tiktok-shop/ ; official policy page to recheck: https://seller-uk.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7753824408307457&lang=en-GB . So the Kit cannot be sold through TikTok Shop UK itself; TikTok can only be used for organic content or ads pointing to our own shop.
3. **Amazon titles have just changed.** From 27 July 2026, Amazon titles in all categories except media must be 75 characters or fewer, with a new 125-character "Item Highlights" field. Any prompt in the Kit that still says "200 characters" for Amazon is out of date. VERIFIED (official): https://sellercentral.amazon.co.uk/seller-forums/discussions/t/33f0a42a-17f1-46ef-b110-ba7512a3c881

## UK law and regulation

On 30 September 2026 the official pages still could not be opened directly, but their own text was returned in web search results from legislation.gov.uk, gov.uk, asa.org.uk and ico.org.uk. The points below are summaries for orientation, not legal advice; link to the official page rather than paraphrasing it in customer copy.

1. **Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013.** Consumers buying from a trader at a distance (online) generally have 14 days to cancel. For digital content not on a tangible medium (such as our Kit download), the trader must not start supplying it within the cancellation period unless the consumer has given express consent and acknowledged that they will lose the right to cancel (regulation 37); once supply begins after that consent and acknowledgement, the right to cancel ends. If the consumer did not give both, or the trader did not send the required confirmation, the consumer pays nothing for content supplied in the cancellation period. Our checkout needs that consent and acknowledgement. These rules apply to traders, not to private individuals selling their own things. VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/37 ; https://www.gov.uk/accepting-returns-and-giving-refunds
2. **Consumer Rights Act 2015.** Goods sold by a trader must be of satisfactory quality, fit for purpose and as described; digital content has equivalent rights. If digital content does not conform, the consumer's remedies are repair or replacement (within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience) and, failing that, a price reduction, which can be the full price (section 42 to 44). There is no "right to reject" for digital content. Faults that appear within 6 months of supply are presumed to have been there at supply. Refunds are due without undue delay and within 14 days of the trader agreeing one is due, by the same payment method. Private sales between individuals are not covered by the CRA in the same way, but goods must still match their description. VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026): https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/42 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/part/1/chapter/3 ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/42/notes?view=plain
3. to 5. **Tax topics removed (owner's instruction, September 2026).** HMRC platform reporting, the trading allowance and the "badges of trade" test are no longer summarised here, and no Well Listed content states tax rules or thresholds. Content points people to gov.uk (search "selling online tax" or "working for yourself") or an accountant instead.
6. **ASA and CAP Code: misleading claims.** Marketing must not materially mislead. CAP Code rule 3.7: before publishing, marketers must hold documentary evidence for claims consumers are likely to see as objective. Testimonials alone are unlikely to be enough evidence; obvious puffery is allowed if it does not materially mislead. This covers our own ads and claims about the Kit. VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026): https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html ; https://www.asa.org.uk/type/capcode/code_rule/3.7.html
7. **ASA and CAP: influencer and affiliate disclosure.** Content with an affiliate link or code counts as advertising and must be obviously identifiable as an ad upfront; the simplest way is a clear "Ad" label (such as "#ad"). "#aff", "affiliate", "#gifted" or "I work with them" on their own are not enough, and the label must not be hidden. The brand and the affiliate are jointly responsible for the ad, even if the brand did not control the content. VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026): https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/affiliate-marketing.html ; https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html ; https://www.asa.org.uk/resource/influencers-guide.html
8. **Fake reviews: DMCC Act 2024.** From April 2025 (the regime started on 6 April 2025) several review practices became "banned practices" under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, automatically unfair and illegal: obtaining or posting fake reviews, paid-for reviews not clearly marked as incentivised, hiding negative reviews, and presenting star ratings misleadingly. The CMA can enforce directly and fine a business up to 10% of global turnover for breaking consumer law. VERIFIED (official, 30 September 2026): https://www.gov.uk/government/news/fake-and-misleading-reviews-5-businesses-under-cma-investigation ; https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-to-boost-consumer-and-business-confidence-as-new-consumer-protection-regime-comes-into-force ; https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67eeb64fe9c76fa33048c790/CMA208_-_Fake_reviews_guidance.pdf
