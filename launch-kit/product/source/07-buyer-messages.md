# 07 Buyer messages

Messages eat evenings. "Is this still available?", the £3 offer on a £20 coat, the parcel stuck in a depot, the return request you are not sure you have to accept.

These prompts help you reply quickly, politely and correctly. They are built on three rules:

1. **Be polite, brief and factual.** You are writing for the buyer and, if it ever gets that far, for the platform's support team reading the thread later.
2. **Stay on the platform.** Every message and every payment goes through the app.
3. **Respect the buyer's rights.** Never refuse a legal right or a platform protection, and never pressure anyone about feedback.

This module is practical guidance, not legal advice. If a dispute is serious, check the platform's help pages, gov.uk or Citizens Advice.

## Privacy: never paste the buyer's personal details into the AI

When you paste a buyer's message into an AI tool, **remove their name, username, address, phone number, email and order number first.** Replace them with [BUYER] or [ORDER]. The AI does not need them to write a good reply, and you should not be sharing someone else's personal data with a third-party tool. Paste your reply back into the app and add the name there if you want to.

Also remove anything personal about you, such as your home address or surname.

## Never move payment off the platform

If a buyer asks to pay by bank transfer, PayPal "friends and family", cash on collection arranged by message, or to "message me on WhatsApp", the answer is no.

- It removes the buyer's protection and yours.
- It is how many scams start.
- eBay's rules, for example, do not allow offers to buy or sell outside eBay, or sharing contact details before a sale is completed. Other platforms have similar rules. Check the current rule in the platform's help pages.

Use the platform's own offer, bundle or private offer tools instead. Template 12 below gives you a ready reply.

## Returns: private sellers and traders are different

This is the part most sellers get wrong, in both directions. Here is the short version. Check the current details on gov.uk or Citizens Advice.

**First, which are you?** If you are clearing out your own things, you are usually a private seller. If you buy items to resell for profit, or make items to sell, you are likely to be a trader (a business) in law, even if you use a personal account. The platform's reporting to HMRC is a separate question from this. If you are not sure, read the guidance on gov.uk.

| | Private seller | Trader (business seller) |
|---|---|---|
| Item must match your description | Yes | Yes |
| Item must be of satisfactory quality and fit for purpose | Not in the same way. The main rule is "as described", so describe faults honestly | Yes, under the Consumer Rights Act 2015 |
| Buyer can reject a faulty item for a refund | Only if it was not as described | Yes, generally within 30 days (the "short-term right to reject") |
| Buyer can cancel an online order for any reason | No legal right to do this | Yes, generally 14 days from the day after delivery, then 14 more days to send it back (Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013). Some exceptions, such as personalised items |
| Refund timing on cancellation | Not applicable | Generally within 14 days of getting the item back or proof it was sent, including the original standard delivery charge |
| Can "no returns" in the listing stop this? | It stops change-of-mind returns | No. A trader cannot remove these legal rights |

**Platform protection sits on top of the law.** Even as a private seller with "no returns", platform buyer protection can still apply:

- **eBay UK:** the eBay Money Back Guarantee lets buyers ask for a refund if an item does not arrive, is faulty or damaged, or does not match the listing, even when the seller does not accept returns. Buyers generally have 30 days from delivery.
- **Vinted Pro:** Vinted is for private second-hand selling, and traders are expected to use Vinted Pro. Vinted's Pro terms require Pro sellers to accept returns within 14 days of receipt.
- **Vinted:** buyers have a short window after Vinted says the parcel should have arrived (2 days at the time of writing) to report an issue under Buyer Protection. If they do not, the order completes.
- **Depop, Etsy, Amazon UK and TikTok Shop UK** each have their own buyer protection and returns rules.

Rules and time limits change. **Check the current rule in the platform's help pages** before you reply to any return or refund request.

The practical upshot: **an honest listing with every flaw described and photographed is your best protection.** If it was as described, you are on firm ground. If it was not, the fair and usually required thing is to sort it out.

### M1 Reply to any buyer message
**Use it when:** you have a message that does not fit the other prompts, or you just want a quick, polite reply.
**Paste this:**
```text
Help me reply to a buyer on [PLATFORM]. I have removed their name and personal details.

My Listing Brief (for facts about the item):
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

I sell as a: [PRIVATE SELLER / BUSINESS]
Buyer's message:
[PASTE MESSAGE, WITH NAMES AND DETAILS REPLACED BY [BUYER]]

What I want to say in my own words (rough is fine):
[E.G. "YES I CAN POST TOMORROW BUT NOT BEFORE"]

Write one reply.

Rules:
- UK English, friendly and brief (under 60 words unless the situation needs more). No emojis unless I ask.
- Only state facts from my brief or my notes. If the buyer asks something I have not answered, write [CHECK: ...] instead of guessing.
- Keep everything on the platform. Never suggest paying, messaging or meeting off the platform.
- Do not promise delivery dates I have not given.
- Do not refuse any legal right or platform protection. If the message is about returns, refunds or a problem with the item, say so and suggest I use the specific prompt for it.
- Do not mention feedback or reviews.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Buyer:** "Hi, does this come from a smoke free home? And can you post Saturday?"
> **Seller notes:** yes smoke free, I post Mondays and Thursdays.
>
> "Hi, thanks for asking. Yes, it comes from a smoke-free home. I post on Mondays and Thursdays, so if you buy before Monday it will go out then. [CHECK: pet-free too? Add if true.]"

**Check before you post:**
- Names, addresses and order numbers were removed before pasting.
- Every fact in the reply is true.

### M2 "Is this still available?"
**Use it when:** you get the classic question, and want a reply that answers it and gently moves things along.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] has asked if my item is still available. Write a short, friendly reply.

My Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Is it still available? [YES / NO, SOLD / ON HOLD FOR SOMEONE ELSE]
When I can post: [E.G. "NEXT WORKING DAY"]
Anything useful to add: [E.G. "HAPPY TO SEND MORE PHOTOS" / "NONE"]

Rules:
- UK English, under 40 words, one clear sentence answering the question first.
- If available, add one useful fact (posting time or one key detail from the brief) and an easy next step using the platform's tools (buy now, make an offer).
- No pressure or fake urgency ("lots of interest", "going fast") unless I have told you it is true.
- Only use facts from my brief.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> "Hi, yes it is still available. It is a UK 6 and I can post the next working day after purchase. Happy to send extra photos if useful."

**Check before you post:**
- The item really is still available on every platform you cross-list on.
- No urgency you cannot back up.

### M3 Lowball offer
**Use it when:** someone offers far below your price and you want to stay polite without giving the item away.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] has made an offer much lower than my price. Help me reply.

My Listing Brief (includes my floor):
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

My list price: £[AMOUNT]
Their offer: £[AMOUNT]
What I want to do: [COUNTER AT £... / DECLINE POLITELY / ASK WHAT THEY HAD IN MIND]
Any true reason that supports my price (optional): [E.G. "SOLD COMPS AROUND £25", "HEAVY TO POST"]

Write two versions: one friendly counter-offer and one polite decline. Each under 50 words.

Rules:
- UK English, warm and calm. Never sarcastic, never lecture the buyer about value.
- Never go below my floor.
- Only give reasons I have supplied. Do not invent other buyers or deadlines.
- Suggest using the platform's offer button for any counter, so the deal stays on the platform.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Le Creuset casserole, list £65, floor £48, offer £20.
>
> **Counter:** "Thanks for the offer. £20 is a bit too low for me, as similar ones have sold for more. I could do £55 if that helps, and I will send it through the offer button."
>
> **Decline:** "Thanks for your interest, but I cannot go that low on this one. You are welcome to make another offer."

**Check before you post:**
- The counter is above your floor.
- Your stated reason is true (you actually looked at comps).

### M4 Bundle request
**Use it when:** a buyer wants several of your items and asks for a deal.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] wants to buy several of my items together. Help me reply with a bundle price.

The items (title, list price and floor for each):
[PASTE ITEMS HERE]

Postage as one parcel: £[AMOUNT OR "NOT SURE"]
Bundle price I am happy with (or ask the AI to use prompt R4 first): £[AMOUNT]
How the platform handles bundles: [E.G. "BUYER ADDS ITEMS TO A BUNDLE AND I SEND AN OFFER" / "NOT SURE"]

Write a reply under 60 words that:
1. Confirms which items (by short name).
2. Gives the bundle price and says whether postage is included.
3. Tells them how to buy using the platform's bundle or offer tool.

Rules: UK English, friendly. Keep it on the platform. Never go below the total of my floors. If the platform process is "NOT SURE", write [CHECK: how bundles work on this app] rather than guessing.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> "Hi, happy to do all three Frugi tops (stripe, dinosaur and navy) for £19 plus postage, all in one parcel. If you add them to a bundle, I will send you an offer for that price."

**Check before you post:**
- The item names match your listings.
- You have checked the combined parcel fits the postage size you quoted.

### M5 Postage questions
**Use it when:** a buyer asks about postage cost, speed, tracking, collection or sending abroad.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] has asked about postage. Help me reply.

My Listing Brief (includes postage details):
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Buyer's question (personal details removed):
[PASTE QUESTION]

My postage facts:
- Service I use: [E.G. "TRACKED 48"]
- Cost to buyer: £[AMOUNT OR "SET BY THE APP"]
- How soon I post after payment: [E.G. "NEXT WORKING DAY"]
- Do I post outside the UK? [YES / NO]
- Do I allow collection? [YES, AND HOW THE PLATFORM HANDLES IT / NO]

Write a reply under 60 words.

Rules:
- UK English. Only give postage facts I have listed. If the answer is not in my facts, write [CHECK: ...].
- Never promise a delivery date. Say when I will post it and the service's usual time, "usually" not "guaranteed".
- If the buyer asks for collection, only offer it if I said yes, and keep payment on the platform.
- Do not suggest undervaluing, mislabelling or marking anything as a gift on customs forms.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Buyer:** "Could you send it first class? I need it for Friday."
>
> "Hi, I post with Tracked 48 as standard, and I can post the next working day after purchase. I cannot promise Friday delivery, sorry. [CHECK: can you offer a faster service, and at what cost?]"

**Check before you post:**
- No promised delivery date.
- Any upgrade cost is added through the platform, not paid separately.

### M6 Delay: late dispatch or parcel stuck in transit
**Use it when:** you are going to post late, or the tracking has stalled and the buyer is asking where their parcel is.
**Paste this:**
```text
Help me update a buyer on [PLATFORM] about a delay.

Type of delay: [I WILL POST LATE / PARCEL STUCK IN TRANSIT / TRACKING SAYS DELIVERED BUT BUYER HAS NOT GOT IT]
Facts:
- Date of sale: [DATE]
- Date posted (if posted): [DATE OR "NOT YET"]
- Service and whether tracked: [E.G. "TRACKED 48, YES"]
- What tracking shows now: [PASTE STATUS, NO TRACKING NUMBER NEEDED]
- What I can do next: [E.G. "POST TOMORROW MORNING" / "OPEN A CARRIER ENQUIRY"]
Buyer's message (personal details removed): [PASTE OR "NONE, I AM MESSAGING FIRST"]

Write a reply under 80 words that:
1. Says sorry once, plainly, without over-explaining.
2. Gives the facts (what has happened, what I am doing next and when).
3. Tells the buyer they are still covered by the platform's buyer protection, without quoting specific time limits.

Rules: UK English. Do not blame the buyer. Do not promise a delivery date. Do not suggest cancelling outside the platform or refunding by another method. If a fact is missing, write [CHECK: ...].
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Situation:** Tracked 48 posted Monday, tracking has not updated since Tuesday at a depot.
>
> "Hi, sorry for the wait. I posted it on Monday with Tracked 48 and the tracking shows it at a depot since Tuesday. I have asked the carrier to look into it and will update you as soon as I hear. Your purchase is still covered by the platform's buyer protection in the meantime."

**Check before you post:**
- Dates are correct.
- If you have promised to follow up, set a reminder on your phone.

### M7 Return request
**Use it when:** a buyer asks to return an item, for any reason.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] has asked to return an item. Help me work out my position and reply.

I sell as a: [PRIVATE SELLER / TRADER (BUSINESS)]
My listing's return policy: [E.G. "NO RETURNS" / "30 DAY RETURNS, BUYER PAYS POSTAGE"]
Platform return or buyer protection rules I have checked today (paste from help pages, or "NOT CHECKED"):
[PASTE RULE TEXT]
My Listing Brief (what I described):
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]
Date delivered: [DATE]
Buyer's reason (personal details removed):
[PASTE MESSAGE]

Do two things.

Part 1, my position (short bullet points):
- Is the reason change of mind, or a claim that the item is faulty, damaged or not as described?
- If I am a trader: remind me that UK buyers generally have a 14-day right to cancel most online purchases, plus the short-term right to reject faulty goods, and that "no returns" cannot remove these. Flag any exceptions only as [CHECK: ...].
- If I am a private seller: explain that change-of-mind returns are up to me and my listing policy, but claims of "not as described", faulty or damaged may be covered by platform buyer protection whatever my policy says.
- Base anything about platform rules only on the text I pasted. If I pasted nothing, tell me to check the platform's help pages before replying.

Part 2, a reply to the buyer (under 80 words): polite, clear on next steps, and using the platform's own return or refund process.

Rules: UK English. This is not legal advice, say so in one line at the end of Part 1. Never tell the buyer they have no rights. Never suggest refunding off the platform. Do not argue about the buyer's reason in the reply.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Situation:** private seller, no returns, Vinted. Buyer: "It is lovely but doesn't fit, can I send it back?"
>
> **Part 1**
> - This is change of mind, not a fault or "not as described".
> - As a private seller, accepting a change-of-mind return is your choice.
> - If the buyer had said the item was not as described, Buyer Protection could apply, so check the listing matched.
> - Not legal advice. Check Vinted's current help pages.
>
> **Part 2**
> "Hi, sorry it did not fit. As I sell privately I do not usually accept returns for fit, which is why I listed the measurements. You might find it resells well on here. Thanks for understanding."

**Check before you post:**
- You are clear whether you are a private seller or a trader.
- You checked the platform's current return and protection rules.
- If the claim is about a fault or description, use M8 instead.

### M8 "Item not as described"
**Use it when:** a buyer says the item is different from the listing, faulty, or has a flaw you did not mention.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] says my item is not as described. Help me respond fairly.

I sell as a: [PRIVATE SELLER / TRADER]
My original listing text (title and description):
[PASTE LISTING TEXT]
What my photos showed: [DESCRIBE, E.G. "FLAW ON LEFT CUFF SHOWN IN PHOTO 4"]
Buyer's complaint (personal details removed), and any photos they describe:
[PASTE MESSAGE]
Platform rules I have checked today (optional): [PASTE OR "NOT CHECKED"]

Do three things:
1. Compare the complaint with my listing, point by point, and say honestly whether the listing covered it, did not cover it, or it is unclear. Do not take my side automatically.
2. Suggest fair options: ask for photos, return for a full refund through the platform, a partial refund through the platform if the buyer wants to keep it, or let the platform decide. Say which fits best.
3. Write a calm reply under 90 words.

Rules: UK English. If the listing did not mention the problem, say so plainly. Never accuse the buyer of lying, even if I suspect it; if I suspect a swapped or damaged return, tell me to keep records and use the platform's process. Never refund or pay outside the platform. Never ask the buyer to change or remove feedback as part of any deal. Not legal advice.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** grey Next jumper. Listing said "small bobbling under arms". Buyer says "there is a hole on the back".
>
> 1. **Bobbling:** covered in the listing and photos. **Hole on the back:** not mentioned anywhere. This is likely a fair claim unless the hole was made after delivery, which you cannot know.
> 2. **Best fit:** offer a return for a full refund through the platform, or a partial refund if they would rather keep it.
>
> 3. "Hi, I am sorry about that. I did not spot a hole when I listed it, so thank you for letting me know. I am happy to accept a return for a full refund through the app, or offer a partial refund if you would prefer to keep it. Just let me know which suits."

**Check before you post:**
- You reread your own listing before replying.
- Any refund goes through the platform so both of you stay covered.
- Add a note to your checklist so the same flaw does not get missed again.

### M9 Damaged in the post
**Use it when:** the buyer says the item arrived broken, crushed or wet.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] says the item arrived damaged. Help me handle it.

Item and how I packed it: [E.G. "GLASS VASE, BUBBLE WRAP, DOUBLE-WALLED BOX"]
Postage service: [E.G. "PLATFORM LABEL" / "MY OWN TRACKED SERVICE, COMPENSATION UP TO £..."]
Buyer's message and what they say their photos show (personal details removed):
[PASTE MESSAGE]
Platform rules I have checked today (optional): [PASTE OR "NOT CHECKED"]

Give me:
1. A short checklist of what to ask the buyer for (photos of the item, the packaging and the label) and what to keep myself (proof of postage, photos of packing if I took any).
2. Who is likely to handle the claim: the platform (for example if I used a platform label) or me with the carrier. Base this on what I told you, and if unsure write [CHECK: ...] and tell me to check the platform's help pages.
3. A calm reply to the buyer under 80 words that asks for photos and explains the next step through the platform.

Rules: UK English. Do not blame the buyer or the carrier in the reply. Do not refuse a refund in the reply. Do not suggest settling off the platform. Not legal advice.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Denby stoneware mug set, bubble wrap, single-wall box, own Tracked 48.
>
> 1. **Ask the buyer for:** photos of the mugs, the outer box (all sides) and the inner wrapping. **Keep:** proof of postage and tracking.
> 2. **Claim:** you used your own service, so you would usually claim from the carrier yourself while the platform handles the buyer's refund request. [CHECK: the carrier's claim rules and time limit.]
> 3. "Hi, I am really sorry it arrived damaged. Could you send photos of the mugs and of the box and packaging, including the outside? That helps me sort it out with the carrier. I will make sure it is put right through the app."

**Check before you post:**
- Buyer photos are requested through the platform's messages, not by email.
- You check the carrier's claim time limit today, not next week.
- Next time: double-walled box and more padding for ceramics.

### M10 Asking for feedback, without pressure
**Use it when:** a sale has gone smoothly and you want to send a thank-you that mentions feedback, fairly.
**Paste this:**
```text
Write a short thank-you message to a buyer on [PLATFORM] after delivery.

Item: [SHORT NAME]
Anything nice and true to mention: [E.G. "HOPE IT FITS WELL" / "NONE"]
Should the message mention feedback? [YES, ONE NEUTRAL LINE / NO]

Rules:
- UK English, under 40 words, warm and simple.
- If feedback is mentioned, one neutral line only, such as inviting them to leave feedback if they have a moment. Never ask for "positive" or "5 star" feedback.
- Never offer or hint at any discount, refund, freebie or reward in exchange for feedback or a review. That is against platform rules (eBay's feedback policy, for example) and UK law on fake and incentivised reviews.
- Never ask the buyer to contact me first "before leaving feedback" in a way that suggests they should not leave honest feedback.
- No emojis unless I ask.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> "Hi, thanks for buying the Clarks boots. I hope they fit well. If you have a moment, feedback is always appreciated. Any questions, just message me here."

**Check before you post:**
- No reward, discount or favour is linked to feedback.
- It is sent once, not repeatedly.

### M11 Difficult or abusive buyer
**Use it when:** a buyer is rude, threatening, demanding things not in the listing, or threatening bad feedback to get a discount.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] is being difficult. Help me respond calmly and protect myself.

What has happened (personal details removed):
[DESCRIBE OR PASTE MESSAGES]
What the listing actually said about the relevant point: [PASTE OR "NOT RELEVANT"]
Has a sale happened? [YES / NO]

Give me:
1. A one-line read of the situation: is this a genuine problem said rudely, a demand for something not in the listing, a threat linked to feedback, or abuse?
2. What to do: reply, reply once then stop, or do not reply. Include using the platform's report and block tools where relevant, and keeping all messages on the platform as a record.
3. If a reply is sensible, one calm reply under 60 words.

Rules:
- UK English. Calm, neutral, never sarcastic. Never insult or threaten the buyer back.
- If the buyer has a genuine complaint about the item, the reply must still offer the platform's proper route (return or refund process). Do not refuse legal rights because the buyer is rude.
- If the buyer is threatening negative feedback to get something not in the listing, tell me that platforms generally treat this as not allowed (eBay calls it feedback extortion) and to report it through the platform. Do not tell me to give in to it.
- If there are threats to my safety, tell me to stop replying, report it to the platform, and contact the police if I feel at risk.
- Never suggest sharing personal details or moving off the platform.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Situation:** after delivery, buyer says "Refund me £10 or I'll leave negative feedback". Item was as described.
>
> 1. **Read:** a threat linked to feedback, to get something not in the listing.
> 2. **Do:** reply once, then report it through the platform's tools. Keep everything in the app's messages.
> 3. "Hi, I am sorry you are not happy. The item was sent as described and photographed. If there is a problem with it, please open a request through the app and it will be looked at properly."

**Check before you post:**
- You have not said anything you would mind the platform's support team reading.
- Screenshots or messages are kept in the app in case of a dispute.

### M12 Suspicious request or off-platform payment
**Use it when:** a buyer asks to pay another way, wants your email or phone number, asks you to post to a different address, or something just feels off.
**Paste this:**
```text
A buyer on [PLATFORM] has sent a request that may be risky. Help me spot the risk and reply.

The request (personal details removed):
[PASTE MESSAGE]

Give me:
1. A plain list of warning signs in the message (for example: paying outside the app, a link to click, "my courier will collect", asking for my email or phone, posting to a different address from the one in the order, overpaying and asking for money back).
2. What to do: reply, decline, report through the platform, or ignore.
3. A short, polite reply that keeps everything on the platform.

Rules: UK English. Do not help me arrange payment, contact or delivery outside the platform. Do not tell me to click any link in the message. Remind me that platform protection generally only covers sales paid for through the platform, and to check the platform's help pages on scams.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Message:** "Can I pay you by bank transfer so we avoid fees? Send me your number."
>
> 1. **Warning signs:** paying outside the app, asking for your phone number before a sale.
> 2. **Do:** decline politely. Report if they push.
> 3. "Hi, thanks, but I only sell through the app so we are both covered. You are welcome to buy or make an offer here."

**Check before you post:**
- You have not shared any contact details.
- You have not clicked any link sent by the buyer.

## Ready-to-adapt reply templates

Short replies for everyday messages. Copy, fill in the [SQUARE BRACKETS], and check every fact is true for your item before sending. They are written to work on any platform.

**1. Still available**
> Hi, yes it is still available. I can post [WHEN] after purchase. Any questions, just ask.

**2. Sold already**
> Hi, sorry, this one has sold. I have a few similar items listed if you would like a look.

**3. More photos**
> Hi, of course. I have added [WHAT] to the listing photos. Let me know if you need anything else.

**4. Measurements**
> Hi, measured flat: pit to pit [X] cm, length [X] cm, sleeve [X] cm. I have added these to the description too.

**5. Friendly counter-offer**
> Thanks for the offer. The lowest I can do is £[AMOUNT]. I will send it through the offer button if that works for you.

**6. Polite decline**
> Thanks for your interest, but I cannot go that low on this one. You are welcome to make another offer.

**7. Bundle price**
> Happy to do [ITEMS] together for £[AMOUNT] [PLUS / INCLUDING] postage, all in one parcel. If you add them to a bundle I will send you an offer.

**8. Postage timing**
> I post [DAYS / NEXT WORKING DAY] with [SERVICE]. It usually takes [TIME] after that, but I cannot guarantee a delivery date.

**9. Posted**
> Hi, your parcel went out today with [SERVICE]. Tracking is in the app. Thanks again.

**10. Late dispatch**
> Hi, I am sorry, I cannot post until [DAY] because [SHORT TRUE REASON]. It will go out then and I will update you here.

**11. Parcel delayed in transit**
> Hi, sorry for the wait. The tracking shows it at [STATUS] since [DAY]. I have asked the carrier to check and will update you as soon as I hear. You are still covered by the app's buyer protection.

**12. Payment off the platform**
> Hi, thanks, but I only sell and take payment through the app, so we are both protected. You are welcome to buy or make an offer here.

**13. Change-of-mind return (private seller, no returns)**
> Hi, sorry it did not suit. As a private seller I do not usually take returns for [FIT / CHANGE OF MIND], which is why I listed [MEASUREMENTS / DETAILS]. Thanks for understanding.

**14. Return accepted**
> Hi, no problem. Please start the return in the app and I will refund you through it once it is back with me. Thanks for letting me know.

**15. Item not as described**
> Hi, I am sorry about that, and thanks for telling me. Could you send a photo of the [PROBLEM] here? I will sort it out through the app.

**16. Damaged in the post**
> Hi, I am really sorry it arrived damaged. Could you send photos of the item and the packaging, including the outside of the box? I will put it right through the app.

**17. Thank you (with neutral feedback line)**
> Hi, thanks for buying the [ITEM]. I hope you enjoy it. If you have a moment, feedback is always appreciated.

**18. Rude message, first reply**
> Hi, I am sorry you are not happy. If there is a problem with the item, please open a request through the app and it will be looked at properly.

**19. Cross-listed item sold elsewhere**
> Hi, I am really sorry, this has just sold on another app and I have removed it. I have cancelled the order through the app so you will be refunded in full.

Template 19 applies only if an order has come in on a second platform. Cancel it through the platform's own process and check its rules, as some platforms count seller cancellations against you.
