# 05 Photos

Buyers cannot pick your item up. Your photos are the closest they get, so they do most of the describing before anyone reads a word.

AI cannot take your photos for you, but it is very good at three jobs:

1. Planning the shots so you do not forget the label, the flaw or the back.
2. Checking your photo set against your Listing Brief, so the pictures and the words agree.
3. Writing captions and alt text quickly.

Every prompt here starts from your Listing Brief (see module 01). Keep it open in your notes app and paste it in where the prompt says.

## The one rule about edited and AI photos

**Your photos must show the actual item you are sending, in its actual condition.**

- Cropping, straightening and a little brightness are normal and fine.
- Removing a stain, a scratch or bobbling with an editing tool is not fine. Nor is changing the colour, adding a background that suggests something is included when it is not, or using an AI-generated "model" image that makes the item look different from the real one.
- Stock photos and photos copied from other listings or brand websites are not fine for second-hand items. They show someone else's item, not yours.

Platforms take this seriously, and rules differ:

| Platform | What their rules say, in short |
|---|---|
| eBay UK | Photos must accurately represent the item. Stock photos are not allowed for used, damaged or defective items. No added borders, text or watermarks. You are responsible for content you create with AI tools. |
| Vinted | Use your own photos showing the item's current condition. No stock, watermarked or found-online images, no collages. Show the whole item first, show labels on branded items, and show any defects. Vinted's catalogue rules also say photos must show the item as it is, without editing. |
| Etsy | Listings must accurately represent what the buyer will receive. Etsy has specific rules on AI-assisted items and how to disclose AI use. |
| TikTok Shop UK | Realistic AI images or video must be labelled, and AI must not make the product look different from reality (texture, features, size). No text or watermarks other than product branding. |
| Depop, Amazon UK | Both require images that accurately show the product. Amazon has strict main image rules (for example a pure white background and only what is included). |

Rules change. **Check the current rule in the platform's help pages before you use any AI editing or background tool.** If in doubt, leave it out.

A misleading photo is also a fast route to an "item not as described" claim, which you will usually lose.

## Quick reference: shot lists by category

Use this as a starting point. Prompt P1 tailors it to your exact item.

| Category | Must-have shots | Nice to have |
|---|---|---|
| Clothing | Front flat or on hanger, back, brand label, size label, care or material label, every flaw close up | Detail of buttons, zips, fabric texture, lining, measurements with tape |
| Shoes and boots | Pair side on, both soles, inside (insole and size), heel backs, toe caps, every scuff | Box if included, laces, top-down view |
| Electronics | Front, back, all ports, serial or model label, screen switched on, every scratch, everything included laid out | Battery health screen, test in use, original box |
| Homeware | Whole item, underside with any maker's mark, every chip or crack, scale (next to a common object) | In a plain room setting, detail of pattern |
| Books and media | Front cover, spine, back cover, copyright page (for edition), any marks, disc surface for CDs and DVDs | Page edges, inscription, dust jacket close up |
| Toys and collectables | Whole item, all parts laid out, box all sides, any maker's mark, every mark or missing part | Batteries compartment, working lights or sound in a short video |
| Handmade | Whole piece, detail of finish, scale, back or underside, every colour option you sell | Packaging, the piece in use |

### P1 Shot list for my item
**Use it when:** you have written the Listing Brief and want a numbered shot list before you pick up your phone.
**Paste this:**
```text
You are an experienced UK marketplace seller helping me plan listing photos.

Here is my Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

I am listing on: [PLATFORM]
Most photos this platform allows (check the app): [NUMBER OR "NOT SURE"]

Write a numbered shot list for this exact item. For each shot give:
1. What to photograph.
2. The angle or distance.
3. Why a buyer needs it (one short line).

Rules:
- Use UK English.
- Base the list only on facts in my brief. If a shot depends on something the brief does not say (for example whether there is a box or a size label), write [CHECK: ...] instead of assuming.
- Put the main (cover) photo first, then the back, then labels, then every flaw listed in my brief, then details.
- Give every flaw in my brief its own shot.
- If I have more shots than the platform allows, mark which ones to combine or drop, and never drop a flaw shot.
- No filters, no editing that changes colour or hides wear. Say so at the end.
- Keep it short enough to read on a phone.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Clarks black leather Chelsea boots, UK 6, light creasing across toes, small scuff on left heel.
>
> 1. **Cover:** pair side on, left boot in front, plain floor, phone at boot height. Shows shape and colour.
> 2. **Front:** both toes towards camera. Shows the toe creasing honestly.
> 3. **Back:** both heels. Shows heel wear and pull tabs.
> 4. **Soles:** both soles side by side, top down. Buyers judge wear from the soles.
> 5. **Inside:** size and brand stamp on insole. Proves size and brand.
> 6. **Flaw:** left heel scuff, close up, with a coin next to it for scale.
> 7. **Flaw:** toe creasing, close up, in daylight.
> 8. **Detail:** elastic side panel, stretched slightly. Shows it has not gone baggy. [CHECK: is the elastic still firm?]
>
> [CHECK: is there a box? If yes, add one shot of it.]
> No filters or editing that changes colour or hides wear.

**Check before you post:**
- Every flaw in your brief has its own photo.
- The cover photo shows the whole item, not a close up.
- Any [CHECK] notes are answered before you shoot.

### P2 Flaw photo checklist
**Use it when:** your item has any wear, marks or faults and you want to show them properly (this protects you as much as the buyer).
**Paste this:**
```text
Help me photograph the flaws on my item honestly and clearly.

Here is my Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Anything else I have noticed since writing the brief:
[EXTRA FLAWS, OR "NONE"]

Do the following:
1. List every flaw mentioned above as a checklist.
2. For each flaw, tell me how to photograph it so it is clearly visible: distance, angle, lighting, and what to put next to it for scale (a coin, a ruler or a tape measure).
3. Suggest a short caption for each flaw photo that matches the wording in my brief exactly.
4. List common places flaws hide on this type of item that my brief does not mention, as questions for me to check (for example "Have you checked the armpits for bobbling?"). Label these [CHECK: ...]. Do not assume they are flawed.

Rules: UK English. Do not describe any flaw as smaller or less noticeable than my brief says. No reassuring words like "barely noticeable" unless my brief uses them. Plain, factual wording only.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** grey Next wool-blend jumper, size 12, small bobbling under arms.
>
> **Flaw checklist**
> - [ ] Bobbling under both arms
>
> **How to shoot it:** lay the jumper flat, lift one arm out to the side, shoot from about 20 cm in daylight from a window. Side light shows bobbling better than light from straight on. Put a ruler along the bobbled patch.
>
> **Caption:** "Small bobbling under both arms, shown close up."
>
> **Also worth checking:**
> - [CHECK: any bobbling on the sides or front where a bag strap would rub?]
> - [CHECK: cuffs stretched or marked?]
> - [CHECK: any small holes? Hold it up to a window.]
> - [CHECK: any smell, such as smoke or perfume? Mention it in the description if so.]

**Check before you post:**
- The flaw is visible at phone screen size, not just when zoomed in.
- Captions use the same words as your description.
- Anything new you find goes into your Listing Brief too.

### P3 Phone lighting and background plan
**Use it when:** you are photographing at home with just a phone and want better, more accurate colour without buying kit.
**Paste this:**
```text
I photograph listings on my phone at home. Help me set up for this item.

Here is my Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

My set-up:
- Phone: [PHONE MODEL, OR "NOT SURE"]
- Time I usually shoot: [E.G. WEEKDAY EVENINGS]
- Space I have: [E.G. SMALL BEDROOM, WINDOW FACING NORTH]
- Things I own that could be a background: [E.G. WHITE DUVET COVER, GREY WALL, WOODEN FLOOR]

Give me:
1. The best place and time to shoot, using the window light I have.
2. Which of my backgrounds suits this item's colour and why.
3. Five phone camera tips in plain English (focus, exposure, lens cleaning, avoiding zoom, avoiding flash glare).
4. How to check the colour in the photo matches the real item before I post.
5. What to do if I must shoot after dark, using only ordinary household lamps.

Rules: UK English, no jargon, short numbered steps. Do not recommend buying anything. Do not suggest any editing that changes the item's colour or hides wear.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** mustard yellow cotton cardigan, size M.
>
> 1. **Where and when:** shoot near your north-facing window in daylight, ideally at the weekend. North light is soft and even, which suits a strong colour like mustard.
> 2. **Background:** the grey wall. White can make mustard look darker than it is, and wood will clash.
> 3. **Tips:** wipe the lens on your top first. Tap the screen on the cardigan to focus. Slide the brightness down slightly if the colour looks washed out. Step closer rather than zooming. Flash off.
> 4. **Colour check:** hold the cardigan next to your phone screen in the same light. If the photo looks more orange or paler, retake it rather than editing the colour.
> 5. **After dark:** turn off the main ceiling light, use two lamps either side at the same height, and say "photographed in lamplight, colour may look slightly warmer" only if it genuinely does.

**Check before you post:**
- The colour on screen matches the item in daylight.
- Nothing in the background could be mistaken as included.

### P4 Photo set audit
**Use it when:** you have taken your photos and want a second pair of eyes to spot gaps before you post.
**Paste this:**
```text
Check my photo set against my Listing Brief before I post.

Here is my Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Here is a plain description of each photo I have taken, in order:
1. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 1, E.G. "FRONT OF JUMPER, FLAT ON BED"]
2. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 2]
3. [ADD MORE LINES AS NEEDED]

(If your AI tool accepts images, you can upload the photos instead. Only upload photos of the item, never anything showing addresses, faces or personal documents.)

Tell me:
1. Missing shots: anything a buyer would expect for this item that is not in my set.
2. Mismatches: anything in the photos that contradicts the brief (for example a flaw shown but not in the brief, or an item in shot that is not included in the sale).
3. Order: the best order for these photos, with the cover photo first.
4. Privacy: anything in the background I should crop out (post, family photos, house number, reflections).

Rules: UK English. Only comment on what I have described or shown. If you cannot tell something, write [CHECK: ...]. Be direct and brief.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Tefal 1.7 litre kettle, white, limescale inside, small chip on lid.
>
> **Missing:** a photo of the base plate and plug, and one of the inside showing the limescale (it is in your brief, so show it).
> **Mismatch:** photo 3 shows two mugs next to the kettle. Buyers may think they are included. Retake without them or say clearly they are not included.
> **Order:** 1 (front), 4 (lid chip close up moved up), 2 (back), then base.
> **Privacy:** photo 2 shows a letter on the worktop. Crop it out or retake.
> [CHECK: does the kettle still boil? If yes, a short video of it boiling helps.]

**Check before you post:**
- Nothing in any photo suggests something is included when it is not.
- No post, faces, number plates or house numbers anywhere in the frame (including reflections in shiny items).

### P5 Photo captions
**Use it when:** your platform lets you add a caption or short text to each photo, or you want matching lines to use in the description.
**Paste this:**
```text
Write one short caption for each of my listing photos.

Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

My photos, in order:
1. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 1]
2. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 2]
3. [ADD MORE AS NEEDED]

Rules:
- UK English, 12 words or fewer per caption.
- Plain and factual. Say what the photo shows, not how great it is.
- Flaw captions must use the same wording as my brief and must not play the flaw down.
- Only use facts from the brief. If a caption needs a fact I have not given, write [CHECK: ...].
- No hype words (stunning, gorgeous, must-have, rare), no emojis, no exclamation marks.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Le Creuset 20 cm cast iron casserole, Volcanic orange, light marks on enamel inside.
>
> 1. Front view, 20 cm casserole in Volcanic orange.
> 2. Lid on, handles both sides shown.
> 3. Inside: light marks on the enamel, see close up.
> 4. Close up of marks on the base of the inside.
> 5. Underside with size stamp. [CHECK: what number is stamped?]
> 6. Next to a 1 litre milk bottle for scale.

**Check before you post:**
- Captions match the description word for word on flaws.
- Nothing says "rare", "vintage" or "authentic" unless you can back it up.

### P6 Alt text for listing photos
**Use it when:** your platform or your own shop has an alt text box (a short written description of an image for people using screen readers). Etsy and most web shops have one.
**Paste this:**
```text
Write alt text for each of my product photos.

Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Photos, in order:
1. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 1]
2. [DESCRIBE PHOTO 2]
3. [ADD MORE AS NEEDED]

Character limit for alt text on my platform (check the help pages): [NUMBER OR "NOT SURE"]

Rules:
- UK English. Describe what is in the image plainly, as if to someone who cannot see it: item, colour, angle, and any flaw shown.
- Do not start with "Image of" or "Photo of".
- Do not stuff keywords or repeat the title. Alt text is for people, not search.
- Only use facts from my brief. If you need a detail I have not given (such as the exact colour), write [CHECK: ...].
- If I have not given a limit, keep each one under 125 characters.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** handmade blue ceramic mug, speckled glaze, 350 ml.
>
> 1. Speckled blue ceramic mug with a curved handle, side view on a wooden table.
> 2. Top view inside the mug showing the pale blue glaze and darker speckles.
> 3. Unglazed base with a stamped maker's mark. [CHECK: what does the mark say?]
> 4. Mug held in a hand to show size, roughly the height of a palm.

**Check before you post:**
- Each line describes the photo, not the sales pitch.
- No list of search terms tacked on the end.

### P7 Is this edit OK?
**Use it when:** you are tempted to use a background remover, a "magic eraser", an AI model or any AI photo tool, and want to check it will not misrepresent the item.
**Paste this:**
```text
I want to edit a listing photo. Tell me whether the edit risks misrepresenting my item.

Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Platform: [PLATFORM]
The original photo shows: [DESCRIBE THE ORIGINAL]
The edit I want to make: [E.G. REPLACE MESSY BEDROOM BACKGROUND WITH PLAIN WHITE / REMOVE A HAIR / PUT JUMPER ON AI MODEL]
The tool I would use: [TOOL NAME OR TYPE]

Answer in three parts:
1. **Risk:** low, medium or high that the edit changes how the item looks (colour, condition, size, shape, what is included). Explain in one or two lines.
2. **Safer option:** a way to get a similar result without editing the item itself (for example reshooting on a plain sheet).
3. **Platform rule:** remind me that I must check the current photo and AI rules in the [PLATFORM] help pages. Do not state specific rules as fact unless I have pasted them in below.

Platform rule text I have copied from the help pages (optional):
[PASTE RULE TEXT, OR "NONE"]

Rules: UK English. Be honest, not reassuring. If the edit would hide or reduce any flaw in my brief, say plainly that I should not do it.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** cream Zara linen shirt, size S, faint mark on left cuff.
> **Edit:** remove background and use an AI model wearing the shirt.
>
> 1. **Risk: high.** An AI model image generates a new version of the shirt. The fit, colour and creases may change, and the cuff mark may disappear. That misrepresents the actual item.
> 2. **Safer option:** hang the shirt on a door against a plain wall, shoot in daylight, and include the cuff close up. If you want to show fit, give chest and length measurements.
> 3. **Platform rule:** check the current photo and AI rules in Vinted's help pages before using any editing tool. If you post edited images, the real flaws must still be clearly visible in your own unedited photos.

**Check before you post:**
- At least one unedited photo of the real item is in the set.
- Every flaw from your brief is still visible after any edit.
- You have checked the platform's current photo rules.

### P8 Choose my cover photo
**Use it when:** you have several decent shots and want to pick the first one buyers see in search.
**Paste this:**
```text
Help me choose the cover (first) photo for my listing.

Listing Brief:
[PASTE YOUR LISTING BRIEF HERE]

Platform: [PLATFORM]
My candidate photos:
A. [DESCRIBE PHOTO A]
B. [DESCRIBE PHOTO B]
C. [DESCRIBE PHOTO C]

Pick one and explain why in two lines. Judge on: the whole item is visible, colour looks true, background is plain, the item fills most of the frame, and it would still be clear as a small square thumbnail on a phone.
Then say what one change would make the winning photo better when I reshoot.

Rules: UK English. Do not suggest adding text, borders, stickers or badges to the photo (many platforms do not allow them). Do not suggest hiding flaws in the cover photo by angle alone if the flaw is major; say so if it is.
```
**What good output looks like:**
> **Item:** Lego Creator 3 in 1 set, built, box included, one missing minifigure.
>
> **Pick: B** (built model on a plain white sheet, front on). The whole model is visible and it reads clearly as a thumbnail. A has the box cut off, and C is too dark.
> **One change:** step back slightly so there is a small gap around the model on all sides, as square crops can cut the edges.
> The missing minifigure is a major point, so show it in photo 2 and put it in the first line of the description.

**Check before you post:**
- The cover photo shows the item you are selling, not a box or a stock image.
- Major flaws are shown early in the photo set, not hidden at the end.
