# How to write an eBay title (UK): a simple formula with examples

**The short answer:** write the words a UK buyer would type, in the order they would type them. Start with the brand, then the item type, then the details buyers filter by (model, size, colour, material). Use most of the space, but only with true, useful words. No hype, no other brand names, no flaws. At the time of writing eBay UK allows up to 80 characters, spaces included, so check the current limit in eBay's help pages before you rely on it.

That is the whole method. The rest of this guide shows how to apply it to real kinds of items, with examples you can copy.

## Why the title matters so much

On eBay, the title is what search matches against and, on a phone, it is often the only text a buyer reads before they tap or scroll past. A good title is not clever. It is plain, specific and accurate.

Two things follow from that:

1. **Words buyers do not type are wasted space.** Nobody searches for "stunning" or "L@@K".
2. **Words that are not true cost you later.** "Vintage" on a 2021 dress or "genuine" on an unchecked bag brings the wrong buyer and, often, a "not as described" return.

## The basic eBay title formula

Start with this order and adjust it for your category.

**Brand + Item type + Model or style + Key details + Size + Colour + Material**

Here are the versions we use for common UK categories:

| Category | Word order |
|---|---|
| Clothing | Brand, gender, item type, style, colour, material, size |
| Shoes | Brand, model, gender, shoe type, colour, UK size |
| Electronics | Brand, model name, model number, capacity, colour, unlocked or boxed |
| Homeware | Brand or maker, range, item type, colour, size or capacity |
| Toys and collectables | Brand, name or set number, series, complete or boxed |
| Books | Title, author, format, edition (only if confirmed) |

Collectors sometimes search differently (set number first, for example). If sold listings in your niche all lead with the same word, follow them.

## eBay title tips that make a real difference

### Use UK words and UK sizes

UK buyers search "jumper", not "sweater". "Trousers", not "pants". "Trainers", not "sneakers". "Handbag", not "purse" (a purse is a small wallet here). For shoes, write "UK 9", not just "9", because EU and US sizes are common on labels.

### Put the important words in the first 40 or so characters

On a phone, long titles are cut off. Make sure the first few words already say what the item is. "Barbour Mens Bedale Wax Jacket" tells a buyer everything that matters before the cut.

### Use the space, but do not pad it

Aim for most of the 80 characters (70 to 80 is a good target) and fill them with real details: neckline, sleeve length, fit, capacity, what is included. If you run out of true details, stop. A 64-character accurate title beats an 80-character padded one.

### Leave flaws out of the title

Flaws belong in the description and the condition field, with sizes and photo numbers. Our [eBay condition description examples](/blog/ebay-condition-description-examples.html) show how to word them.

### Never borrow another brand

"Zara style", "not Mulberry" and "like Nike" are keyword spam and can break eBay's rules on search manipulation. List only the brand on the label. "Unbranded" is fine and honest.

### Skip the things eBay already shows

"Free postage", "Buy it now" and prices waste characters when eBay already displays them next to the listing.

## eBay listing title examples: before and after

All items below are invented examples. Character counts include spaces.

**Women's jumper**

Before: `Lovely grey jumper size 12 VGC must have`

After: `Next Womens Grey Wool Blend Crew Neck Jumper Size 12 Long Sleeve Knit Pullover` (78)

The "after" leads with brand, says what it is, and adds the details buyers filter by. "VGC" moves into the condition field and description.

**Men's trainers**

Before: `STUNNING Nike Air Max 90 trainers!!! L@@K like new rare` (55)

After: `Nike Air Max 90 Mens Trainers White Grey UK 9 Leather Lace Up Running Shoes` (75)

Out go capitals, exclamation marks, "stunning" and "rare". In go colour, UK size and material. "Like new" is a condition claim, so it belongs in the condition field, and only if it is true.

**Wax jacket**

`Barbour Mens Bedale Wax Jacket Olive Green Size 40 Cord Collar Tartan Lining` (76)

**Casserole dish**

`Le Creuset Cast Iron Round Casserole Dish 24cm Volcanic Orange 4.2 Litre` (72)

Only use a capacity or colour name if it is on the item, the base or the box. If you are not sure, leave it out.

**Games console**

`Sony PS4 Slim 500GB Console Jet Black CUH-2216A Controller Cables Tested Working` (80)

"Tested working" is only allowed if you really tested it. Say what you tested in the description.

## Counting to 80 characters

Spaces and punctuation count. AI tools are often a few characters out when they count, so always paste your title into the eBay listing form and trust eBay's counter. If you are over, cut the weakest detail first (usually a second style word), not the brand or size.

## A copy-paste prompt for eBay titles

If you use a free AI tool such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot, the trick is to give it facts and forbid it from guessing. This is a short version of the title prompt from our kit:

```text

- UK English and UK sizes ("UK 8" for shoes, "trousers" not "pants").

- Do not mention any brand other than the item's own.
```

## FAQ

**How many characters can an eBay UK title have?**
At the time of writing, 80 characters including spaces. Limits can change, so check the current rule in eBay's help pages or simply watch the counter in the listing form.

**Should I write my eBay title in capitals?**
No. Whole words in capitals read as shouting and use no fewer characters. Capitalise the first letter of each word if you like, which is common on eBay and easy to read.

**Should I put "VGC" or "BNWT" in the title?**
Condition has its own field on eBay, so the title space is usually better spent on brand, size and details. If you do use an abbreviation, make sure it is accurate and repeat the full condition in the description.
