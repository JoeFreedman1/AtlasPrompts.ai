# How to write an eBay title (UK): a simple formula with examples

**The short answer:** write the words a UK buyer would type, in the order they would type them. Start with the brand, then the item type, then the details buyers filter by (model, size, colour, material). Use most of the space, but only with true, useful words. No hype, no other brand names, no flaws. At the time of writing eBay UK allows up to 80 characters, spaces included, so check the current limit in eBay's help pages before you rely on it.

## Why the title matters so much

The title is what eBay search matches against and, on a phone, often the only text a buyer reads before tapping or scrolling past. So words buyers do not type ("stunning", "L@@K") are wasted space, and words that are not true ("vintage" on a 2021 dress) bring the wrong buyer and, often, a "not as described" return.

## The basic eBay title formula

Start with this order and adjust it for your category.

**Brand + Item type + Model or style + Key details + Size + Colour + Material**

Versions for common UK categories:

| Category | Word order |
|---|---|
| Clothing | Brand, gender, item type, style, colour, material, size |
| Shoes | Brand, model, gender, shoe type, colour, UK size |
| Electronics | Brand, model name, model number, capacity, colour, unlocked or boxed |
| Homeware | Brand or maker, range, item type, colour, size or capacity |
| Toys and collectables | Brand, name or set number, series, complete or boxed |

If sold listings in your niche all lead with the same word (collectors often start with a set number), follow them.

## eBay title tips that make a real difference

### Use UK words and UK sizes

UK buyers search "jumper", not "sweater", and "trousers", "trainers" and "handbag" rather than "pants", "sneakers" and "purse". For shoes, write "UK 9", not just "9", because labels often carry EU and US sizes too.

### Put the important words in the first 40 or so characters

Long titles get cut off on a phone, so the first few words must say what the item is. "Barbour Mens Bedale Wax Jacket" does that before the cut.

### Use the space, but do not pad it

Fill the space with real details, not padding: neckline, sleeve length, fit, capacity, what is included. If you run out of true details, stop. An accurate 64-character title beats a padded 80-character one.

### Leave flaws out of the title

Flaws go in the description and condition field, with sizes and photo numbers (see our [eBay condition description examples](/blog/ebay-condition-description-examples.html)).

### Never borrow another brand

"Zara style", "not Mulberry" and "like Nike" are keyword spam and can break eBay's rules on search manipulation. List only the brand on the label. "Unbranded" is fine and honest.

## eBay listing title examples: before and after

All items below are invented examples. Character counts include spaces.

**Women's jumper**

Before: `Lovely grey jumper size 12 VGC must have`

After: `Next Womens Grey Wool Blend Crew Neck Jumper Size 12 Long Sleeve Knit Pullover` (78)

Brand first, then the details buyers filter by. "VGC" moves to the condition field.

**Men's trainers**

Before: `STUNNING Nike Air Max 90 trainers!!! L@@K like new rare` (55)

After: `Nike Air Max 90 Mens Trainers White Grey UK 9 Leather Lace Up Running Shoes` (75)

Out go capitals, "stunning" and "rare". In go colour, UK size and material. "Like new" belongs in the condition field, and only if true.

**Wax jacket**

`Barbour Mens Bedale Wax Jacket Olive Green Size 40 Cord Collar Tartan Lining` (76)

**Games console**

`Sony PS4 Slim 500GB Console Jet Black CUH-2216A Controller Cables Tested Working` (80)

Only write "tested working" if you really tested it, and say how in the description.

## Counting to 80 characters

Spaces and punctuation count, and AI tools often miscount by a few. Paste your title into the eBay form and trust eBay's counter. If you are over, cut the weakest detail (usually a second style word), never the brand or size.

## FAQ

**How many characters can an eBay UK title have?**
At the time of writing, 80 characters including spaces. Check the current rule in eBay's help pages, or watch the counter in the listing form.

**Should I write my eBay title in capitals?**
No. Whole words in capitals read as shouting. Capitalising the first letter of each word is common on eBay and easy to read.

**Should I put "VGC" or "BNWT" in the title?**
Condition has its own field, so title space is usually better spent on brand, size and details. If you do use one, make sure it is accurate and spell out the condition in the description.
