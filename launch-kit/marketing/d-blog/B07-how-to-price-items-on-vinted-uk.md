# How to price items on Vinted UK (without guessing)

To price items on Vinted UK, search for the same brand, item type and size, and note 5 to 10 genuinely similar listings, giving most weight to any you can see have sold. Find the middle price, adjust up or down for your item's condition, then list a little above that middle to leave room for offers. Decide your lowest acceptable price (your floor) before you post, and remember the buyer pays postage and a Buyer Protection fee on top of your price.

The rest of this Vinted pricing guide covers each step.

## Why guessing costs you

Too high, and the item sits collecting favourites. Too low, and it goes in an hour and you wonder what you left on the table. The fix is to price from similar items, and to be strict about what counts as similar.

## Step 1: find your comparable items by hand

Search Vinted the way a buyer would (brand, item type, size), then filter by size and condition.

Copy the details of 5 to 10 matches into your notes app: title, price, condition and size. If you can see an item is marked as sold, note that, because a sold price is evidence and an asking price is only hope.

For branded items, eBay's "Sold items" filter is a useful second opinion.

Do it by hand. Scraping tools, bots and add-ons that harvest listings are often against platforms' terms (check Vinted's), and ten minutes of looking gives better comps anyway, because you can see which ones actually match.

### What makes a good comp

| Good comp | Weak comp |
|---|---|
| Same brand and style | "Similar" items from another brand |
| Same size | One size up or down (matters a lot for children's clothes and shoes) |
| Similar condition | New with tags against your worn item |
| Recent | Listed or sold a long time ago |
| Marked as sold | Asking price on something that has sat for months |

## Step 2: find the middle, not the top

Put your Good matches in order and take the middle one (the median). Ignore the single highest listing: that seller may still be waiting.

Worked example (invented figures for illustration): a Boden navy cord skirt, size 10, good condition, one small mark on the back hem. Good matches: £9, £11, £12, £14, £16. The middle is £12.

## Step 3: adjust for your item's condition

The mark on the hem puts our skirt slightly below the cleanest comps, so we call it £11 to £12. Tags still on would sit above the middle. Missing belts, bobbling and fading nudge you down. Whatever you decide, the flaw goes in the description and photos.

## Step 4: leave room for offers, then set a floor

Vinted buyers make offers, so list a little above your value and decide your floor now, while you are calm.

For the skirt: list at £14, expect offers around £11 to £12, floor £10. When the £6 offer arrives, you already know the answer. Our guide on [how to respond to lowball offers on Vinted](/blog/how-to-respond-to-lowball-offers-vinted.html) has the replies.

## Step 5: think about what the buyer actually pays

Vinted adds a mandatory Buyer Protection fee at checkout, paid by the buyer. It is a percentage of the item price plus a fixed amount, and Vinted says it varies. Postage goes on top too. So a £14 skirt costs the buyer noticeably more than £14 by the time they pay. On low-value items, that is often why a bundle sells when a single item does not. Check the current fee in Vinted's help pages.

## How much to sell clothes for on Vinted

There is no honest percentage-of-retail rule. Brand, condition, size and season matter more than the original price tag. Coats and knitwear tend to move better as the weather turns; linen and sandals as it warms up. The comps are the answer, so recheck them if something has been listed a while.

## Vinted price drops and favourites

Lots of favourites and no sales usually means buyers like the item but something is holding them back. Before you cut the price, check:

- Is the cover photo the whole item, flat, in daylight?
- Are measurements in the description (pit to pit, length)? See our [Vinted description template](/blog/vinted-description-template.html).
- Is the title clear: brand, item, colour, size?

Then drop in planned steps rather than panic. For the skirt: £14 now, £12 after two weeks, £10 (the floor) after four, then stop. If the app lets you send an offer to people who favourited the item, that is worth a try at each step. Check how offers and favourites currently work in the Vinted app, as features change.

If it still has not sold at the floor, bundle it, relist it next season with fresh photos, or donate it.

Never ask an AI "what is this worth?" on its own. It does not know what sold last week.

## FAQ

**Is a suggested price in the app accurate?**
Treat any suggestion as a starting point only. Your own comps are better evidence.

**Should I price high and wait?**
Only a little above your value. Far above the comps, most items just sit.

**When should I drop my price on Vinted?**
After you have fixed the photos, title and measurements, and on a plan you decided in advance. Stop at your floor.
